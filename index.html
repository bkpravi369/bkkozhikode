🕊️ Who We Are · ആരാണ് ബ്രഹ്മാകുമാരീസ്?

A Worldwide Spiritual Movement

Brahma Kumaris, officially known as ‘Prajapita Brahma Kumaris Ishwariya Vishwa Vidyalaya’, is a worldwide spiritual movement dedicated to personal transformation and world renewal. Founded in 1937 in Hyderabad, Sindh (then a part of undivided India) by Prajapita Brahma, Brahma Kumaris has spread to over 130 countries in 5 continents of the world and has had an extensive impact on many sectors as an international NGO.

👑

A Women-Led Spiritual Family

It supports the cultivation of a deep collective consciousness of peace and of the individual dignity of each soul. The Brahma Kumaris has the unique distinction of being the world’s largest spiritual organisation led by women. It was the founder, Prajapita Brahma Baba, who put women in front from the very beginning, appointing Mateshwari Jagdamba, fondly known as Mamma, as its first administrative head.

🏔️

From Mount Abu to the World

In 1950, this spiritual group relocated to India, and found place in the beautiful Aravalli Ranges of Mount Abu, Rajasthan, India, which serves as the international headquarters (Madhuban). From here, the teachings of Rajyoga spread overseas, with the first foreign center established in London in 1971.

⏳ Historical Milestones · ചരിത്ര നാൾവഴികൾ

Our Journey

From a humble beginning to becoming a World Spiritual Organization, the journey of Brahma Kumaris has been a unique one. Explore the 12 pivotal eras that shaped this global movement.

1935

1935 — Unexpected Turn of Events

Dada Lekhraj was the son of a schoolmaster. Although from a middle class family, with honesty and hard work he rose to become one of the richest men in India. He was a diamond merchant of international repute. In 1935, when he was around sixty years of age, he began having divine visions of the Supreme Soul Shiva and world renewal, prompting him to dedicate his life entirely to Godly service.

1936 – 37

1936 – 37 — Embarkation on the New Path

Subsequent to the revelations and having heard the secret voice of God, Dada started sharing the knowledge with his friends and relatives. They began congregating for satsang (spiritual gatherings), which came to be known as Om Mandali. Dada Lekhraj constituted a managing committee of eight young women, headed by Om Radhe (Mateshwari Saraswati Mamma), and surrendered all his worldly fortune to this spiritual trust.

1938 – 39

1938 – 39 — Opposition on the road less travelled

A group of the community was against the Om Mandali and at any cost wanted to cease its operations. This group, called the Anti-Om Mandali Committee, subjected the spiritual gathering to immense pressure. However, the spiritual seekers remained steadfast in their vow of purity, celibacy, and intense remembrance of God.

1940 – 50

1940 – 50 — New Premises, Same Routine

The members of the institution, in Karachi (now in Pakistan) were involved in intense meditation and deep spiritual study. They were around 300 in number. For 14 years, this dedicated group underwent deep spiritual tapasya, refining their character and acquiring spiritual power.

1951 – 60

1951 – 60 — Glorious Propagation - The Planting of a Sapling

In 1950, the institution relocated to Mount Abu, Rajasthan, India, which became its international headquarters (Madhuban). After empowering themselves with deep self-realization, young Brahma Kumari sisters set out in batches to establish spiritual service centers across India. In 1954, a delegation attended the World Religious Conference in Japan.

1961 – 70

1961 – 70 — The Turning Point

This period was witness to the ascension of two extraordinary great souls: Mateshwari Jagdamba (fondly known as Mamma), the first Administrative Head, on 24 June 1965; and Prajapita Brahma Baba, who attained his complete (Sampoorna) stage of perfection on 18 January 1969. The administration was entrusted to Rajyogini Dadi Prakashmani and Didi Manmohini.

1971 – 80

1971 – 80 — BK Services Spread Far and Wide

Guided directly by the Supreme Soul and spearheaded by Rajyogini Dadi Janki, BK services expanded overseas. In 1971, the first center outside India opened in London. Within a few years, centres were established across the UK, Europe, the Americas, Australia, and Africa.

1981 – 90

1981 – 90 — Institution Gains National and International Recognition

Raja Yoga Centers were established in around 40 countries. In 1980, Brahma Kumaris was affiliated to the United Nations Department of Public Information (DPI). The institution received seven UN Peace Messenger Awards for initiatives including The Million Minutes of Peace (1986) and Global Cooperation for a Better World (1988).

1991 – 2000

1991 – 2000 — Emergence of Brahma Kumaris Internationally

In 1991, the multi-specialty Global Hospital and Research Centre was commissioned at Mount Abu. In 1998, Brahma Kumaris was granted General Consultative Status with the UN Economic and Social Council (ECOSOC). Major retreat centers like Peace Village (New York) were established.

2001 – 2010

2001 – 2010 — Important Milestones In Services of BK

The UN designated Brahma Kumaris as Messenger for Manifesto 2000. Iconic spiritual retreat centres like Shanti Sarovar (Hyderabad, 2004) and Om Shanti Retreat Centre (Gurugram) were inaugurated, hosting mega peace conferences for hundreds of thousands of people.

2011 – 2020

2011 – 2020 — Institution Soars in Education, Healthcare, Media Sectors and Rural Area

In 2011, the Platinum Jubilee (75 years) of the organization was celebrated. Community Radio Station Radio Madhuban 90.4 FM was launched. Large retreat campuses like Jagdamba Bhawan (Pune) were established, along with major national campaigns in value education and rural welfare.

2021 – Present

2021 – Until the Present — Changes in the Administrative Heads

The Brahma Kumaris continues to expand its reach across 130+ nations. Following the angelic ascension of Dadi Hridaymohini (2021) and Dadi Ratanmohini (2024), current leadership under Administrative Head BK Mohini Didi and Additional Heads guides millions toward inner peace and positive self-transformation.
